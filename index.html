Research

My current research direction is focused on precision tests of fundamental symmetries in nuclear beta decay.

Experimental and Computational Search for Time-Reversal Violation in Radiative Nuclear Beta Decay

This project explores how a T-odd momentum correlation in radiative beta decay could be used in a precision search for time-reversal violation. The proposed experimental concept involves detecting beta particles, photons, and nuclear recoil while using timing and coincidence information to reconstruct the relevant correlation and search for an asymmetry.

An important part of the project is separating a possible time-reversal signal from electromagnetic final-state interactions and detector-related false asymmetries.

Muon Lifetime / Beamline for Schools Work

I served as the theoretical lead for a high-school team working on a proposal concerning muon lifetime measurements. The proposal was scientifically accepted in CERN's Beamline for Schools competition, although our team was not selected to travel to CERN for the experiment.

This experience strengthened my interest in experimental particle physics, relativistic effects, measurement, and scientific collaboration.
